Ban all mining on Aravalli

Author: Alia Bajaj | Date: January 23, 2026

The 1992 landmark of the supreme court

In 1992 the supreme court of India passed a landmark that banned all mining on the Aravalli hill range but it only applied to Aravalli hills which were 100m or above that in height. More than 90% of the hills in Aravalli range are below 100m and were not provided with legal protection whereas only 8- 9 % of hills in the range were above 100m which means that mining was permitted on most of the hills with a government on SC approved lease.

Mining leases permitted

After the order in 1992 many more leases were permitted which led to 31 % of harm done to the Aravalli’s till date. Most of the hills have been flattened, hollowed out or the thick layer of trees is thinned. Mining leases in India are permitted for 20 – 30 years and can be renewed if permitted. With mining leases permitted for such a long period of time, a lot of harm is done and will be done to the Aravalli’s if not taken care of.

Public reaction

After the new landmark passed by the supreme court the public had a very mixed reaction. Environmentalists and activists took this as the first step to save the Aravalli’s and celebrated the new landmark with joy, but some companies and mine workers started protesting that banning mining on the Aravalli’s will destroy livelihood and cause damage to the economy. The central government supported the courts ecological reasoning while the state government saw it as unemployment for the people and made several failed attempts to remove the ban.

2025 New order by the supreme court

By the end of 2025 the supreme court has now passed a new order regarding the matter. The supreme court banned mining from any hill in Aravalli and banned any further permission for mining lease on Aravalli in the future. The court also stated that no new mining leases will be permitted but existed leases will continue till their expiry with no renewals.

Existing leases

Even after the new order passed there were still a lot of old leases which could do a lot of harm to the hill range. The list of old leases and their expiry have not been published with the public but it is estimated that most of them could end in the early 2030 but some of them can last even longer meaning more harm to the Aravalli range. There are still a lot of existing leases like Rajasthan has about 1200 existing mining leases.

Effects of mining on the Aravalli’s

Mining is a process in which the land is dug to find precious minerals but it is also one of the biggest dangers to the environment. It causes erosion, loss of minerals in the soil and can sometimes also flatten out hills or mountains. The Aravalli’s have lost most of their biodiversity, greenery and the fresh air that once was there because of mining done by us humans for materialistic possession. The thick layers of greenery have thinned, some hills have been flattened, soil erosion is increasing, the fresh is getting polluted and Aravalli is losing its biodiversity. Mining has done 31% harm to the Aravalli’s now even if the existing leases continue it could cause 5- 10 % of more damage. Even the existing leases till there expiry date can harm the hills and provide more damage if we don’t stop now.

The January 21st order by the SC

On January 21st the supreme court released a new order putting an absolute halt to illegal mining on the Aravalli’s and the controversial 100m rule is now officially suspended. The supreme court has received assurance from the Rajasthan government to stop all illegal mining on Aravalli. The court has blocked all new mining and mining renewal requests. The court has also decided to create a new committee of independent experts that will ensure that all illegal mining on the hills is stopped and that the range is redefined scientifically to prevent irreversible damage.

We need to act now

Humans have been blinded with greed for more money and power that we are not realizing what we are doing to the world, to the environment which gave us survival, which fulfilled all our needs and gave us a place to live. We are doing other things than destroying the Aravalli which is equally destroying the environment which helped us grow and reach so far in life. We must always remember that we are dependent on nature and that the environment doesn’t need us to survive. By destroying the Aravalli’s we are forcing other living beings out of their homes making them homeless and destroying natural beauty.
